# About me

I am a phonetician, phonologist, and speech scientist currently serving as a Postdoctoral Fellow in the [NeSST Lab](https://www.nesstlab.com/) at the University of Missouri, in the department of Speech, Language, and Hearing Sciences. Prior to this, I taught linguistics at [Boston College](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/morrissey/departments/eastern-slavic-german/undergraduate/linguistics.html) and [Carleton College](https://www.carleton.edu/linguistics/), and worked as a postdoctoral researcher in English Language & Linguistics at Heinrich-Heine University in Düsseldorf, Germany ([English](https://www.anglistik3.hhu.de/en/), [German](https://www.anglistik3.hhu.de/)), as part of the [Speech, Language, and Modeling Lab](https://slam.phil.hhu.de/).

I completed my Ph.D. at Yale University’s [Department of Linguistics](https://ling.yale.edu/) and its Phonetics Laboratory, with a dissertation on the role of tone in articulatory timing in Tibetan as spoken in diaspora. I am interested in the temporal representation of articulatory gestures, and more generally the relationships between tones and segments, language contact, sound change, and anything related to Tibetan.

To these ends, I have used a variety of methods, including field interviews, corpus methods, acoustic phonetics, and experiments with Electromagnetic Articulography (EMA). With NeSST Lab colleagues, I am studying how speech motor control is affected in speakers with cerebellar ataxia.  

First and foremost, however, I think of myself as a teacher. I approach teaching and mentorship as skills that require continual development like any other, and I do what I can to continue learning and growing as a teacher. This has also led me to involvement in the [Scholarly Teaching](https://www.lsadc.org/af_membercommittee.asp?committeeid=34) and [Linguistics in Higher Ed](https://www.lsadc.org/AF_MemberCommittee.asp?committeeid=15) committees of the Linguistic Society of America. I'm always excited to talk about [Backward Design](https://cteresources.bc.edu/documentation/backwards-design/), pedagogy research, and [Alternative Grading](https://www.centerforgradingreform.org/).
